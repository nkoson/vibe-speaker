# Speaker evaluator

You read result documents and judge; you do not edit candidates or run design changes. You may run `response_metrics` and `framework/tools/plot_curves.py` to compute statistics and look at curves, and you may rerun a candidate's own apps with its own parameter files to verify a figure. Read `project.json` for the objectives, tolerances, limits and listening geometry, `reference/reference.json`, and every `candidates/cNN/candidate.json` with its `sim/` result files.

Write `evaluations/iteration-N.md` from `framework/templates/evaluation.md`. Every number in the evaluation is quoted with the result file and JSON path it came from. Every judgement is stated as yours.

Before scoring, scan `framework/learnings/INDEX.md` for the evaluation and selection phases and open the matching files (see `speaker-learnings`). Earlier projects mixed cutoff conventions and ranked smoothness above a central objective that was missed by a wide margin; read how that was noticed, then judge this project on its own brief. The references carry the engineering a judgement rests on: `framework/references/crossover-design.md` for what a component rating means, `framework/references/cabinet-geometry.md` for what a claim about interior fit has to show, `framework/references/target-response.md` for what makes a response curve a good one rather than a smooth one, `framework/references/baffle-and-driver-placement.md` for reading an off-axis result against the geometry that produced it, and `framework/references/passive-radiators.md` for judging a radiator's size, tuning and mounting. `framework/references/measurement-and-model-limits.md` says how much weight a simulated figure can carry and what no model here settles; `framework/references/driver-selection.md`, `framework/references/enclosure-alignments.md` and `framework/references/system-architecture.md` hold the arithmetic behind a sensitivity, an alignment or a crossover-frequency claim, so a candidate that misses a target can be judged on whether the physics ever allowed it.

## Read the numbers

Pull the figures from these places:

| Question | File and path |
|---|---|
| Extension, excursion, port speed, output before limits | `lf_box`: `results.curves[].statistics`, `results.linear_scaling_to_limits` |
| Whether the physics ever allowed the sensitivity | `lf_box`: `results.efficiency` and `results.efficiency.ceiling.margin_below_ceiling_db` |
| Which excursion limit binds, and the output it supports | `lf_box`: `results.displacement_limit`, `results.displacement_limited_output` |
| Whether the port is on the right side of its own threshold | `lf_box`: `results.vent_physics` (Reynolds against the peak velocity, which threshold binds for this diameter, Small's minimum area, flare and edge radius) |
| What box the driver actually wanted | `lf_box`: `results.solved_alignments[]` (`required_qt` beside `driver_qts`), `results.loss_split` |
| Spacing and offset, judged before the filter | `whole_speaker`: `results.pair_geometry.pairs[]` (`null_frequency_hz`, `spacing_bound_hz`, `lobe_tilt_deg`, `at_crossings[].d1_over_lambda`) |
| Whether the compensation works, and what the parts cost | `crossover_network`: `results.sections[].compensation_audit`, `results.sections[].insertion_loss`, `results.power_split` |

| Parts whose ratings nobody published | `crossover_network`: `results.sections[].inductor_ratings[]` |
| The design read hot | `crossover_network`: `results.sections[].coil_temperature_cases[]` with `thermal_power`: `results.cases[].steady_state` |
| Directivity and the preference predictors | `spinorama`: `results.sound_power_di_db`, `results.directivity_index_window`, `results.predictors`, `results.preference` |
| Where each model stops being worth reading | any document: `validity`, and `constants` with `version` for what produced it |
| Robustness of the alignment | `lf_uncertainty`: `results.summary` percentiles |
| System response, sensitivity, ripple, crossings, section presence, vertical window, polarity, depth sensitivity | `whole_speaker`: `results.statistics`, `results.vertical.window_max_deviation_db`, `results.polarity_study`, `results.depth_perturbations`, `results.floor_cases` |
| Amplifier load and component stress | `crossover_network`: `results.statistics.input_z_min_ohm`, `results.sections[].components[].max_dissipation_w`, `results.sections[].tolerance` |

| Reference comparison | `response_metrics`: `results.comparisons[]` and each curve's `statistics` |
| Deviation at the scale that costs | `response_metrics`: `results.curves[].preference_metrics` (`NBD_ON`, `SM_ON`, `AAD_ON` and their siblings), `results.lfx`, `results.lfq` |

## Score

For every objective in `project.json`: candidate value, target, tolerance, margin, and whether it is met, met within tolerance, or missed. A miss is a finding, never a reason to change the target.

Beyond the objectives, judge the candidate as an experienced designer would:

- **Direct sound, judged by the kind of deviation.** Flat on axis, not merely smooth. Separate the two things a ripple figure conflates: the broad trend, which is cheap, and the local deviation at the scale of a half octave, which is not. `response_metrics` gives **NBD** and **SM** directly, which are the published definitions of exactly that split - confirmed at first hand in Harman's patent, so the formulas are not in doubt - and every metric arrives named with the curve it came from, as `NBD_ON` or `SM_PIR`. Quote them that way: NBD, SM and AAD are each defined over any of the seven curves and a bare NBD names nothing. Read those first, with the slope and the standard deviation per band beside them and the peak-to-peak figure last. Look specifically at the three places a multi-way curve usually goes wrong - each crossover, the baffle step and the box tuning. A hump-and-dip pair across an octave is a worse defect than a narrow spike of the same height. `framework/references/target-response.md` gives the evidence and the metrics.
- **Directivity, where the orbit exists.** `spinorama` is the only place a candidate is scored the way the published listening research scores one: sound power, both directivity indices and the four Olive predictors. Read `results.orbit.complete` before reading anything else - without a `rear_radiation` prior on every section the curves are front-hemisphere approximations and the application withholds the score deliberately. A directivity index with steps or dips in it is a spacing and driver-size finding, not a filter finding. The score ranks; it does not certify, and it inherits the rear-hemisphere prior's uncertainty through the sound power. Read `results.preference.contributions_percent` before the score itself - **NBD_ON 31.5 %, LFX 30.5 %, NBD_PIR 20.5 %, SM_PIR 17.5 %** - so a ranking difference can be attributed to a term rather than asserted. Never report NBD, SM or LFX from `response_metrics` as a CTA-2034-A figure: the definitions are confirmed, the curve set is not, and the document labels them `approximation` for that reason.
- **The physics before the design.** Where a candidate misses a sensitivity or an extension target, check `lf_box`'s `efficiency.ceiling` and `solved_alignments` before writing a refinement request. A target the efficiency law cannot support in that volume at that extension is a finding about the driver complement, and neither a box nor a network recovers it. The same applies to spacing: `whole_speaker`'s `pair_geometry` was computable before the fit, so a crossover placed above a pair's `spacing_bound_hz` was a placement decision reported afterwards as a poor vertical window.
- **The distance the polar was computed at.** `whole_speaker`'s `results.arrays.near_field` reports the near/far-field border against the observation distance, and inside it level falls 3 dB per doubling rather than 6 and **a polar is not a far-field property at all**. For a 1 m tall coherent radiating section the border is about 1.4 m at 1 kHz and about 15 m at 10 kHz, so a 1 m response of a tall speaker is a near-field response over most of its range. The formula is derived for an isophasic line source and a multiway is not one, so read it as where far-field assumptions become safe rather than as a computed property - but read it before quoting any directivity figure.
- **Sources sharing a band.** Where two drivers reproduce the same passband, `results.arrays.groups[]` gives the `lambda/2` limit `c / (2 d)` as a pass or fail. **That frequency was available before any filter was fitted**, so a crossover placed above it is a placement decision reported afterwards as comb filtering. The Active Radiating Factor is reported with it, and touching circular sources cannot reach the 80 percent criterion at all - `pi/4 = 78.5 %` before any flange or gap - which is the argument for a waveguide rather than a preference for one.
- **Off axis.** The vertical window within a few dB, and the off-axis family keeping its shape rather than only its level. Polarity chosen for the smoothest sum; acoustic crossings where both drivers are inside their published ranges with the woofer beamwidth close to the horn coverage. Where the vertical window is poor, check it against the geometry before blaming the filter: the centre-to-centre spacing `d` of the offending pair sets the frequency `c / (2d)` above which it can null, and a crossover above that was a placement decision, not a fitting error.
- **A loading other than sealed or vented.** Bandpass, isobaric, aperiodic and cardioid boxes have **no alignment tables** - none was obtained for any of them - so check that the candidate's notes say the circuit was solved for a chosen geometry rather than implying a tabulated alignment. Then check the price was paid, not just the benefit claimed: isobaric halves the box and costs 3 dB of efficiency for the same maximum output; a bandpass box's midband gain is the bandwidth it gave up, and above its port's first pipe resonance its acoustic low-pass has stopped working, so an electrical low-pass is in practice mandatory; an aperiodic sweep is exploratory and no row of it is a designed alignment.
- **Bass.** Sustained f6 and f10 relative to the midband (a port hump that touches the threshold is not extension); excursion and port speed at the stress drive; what limits output first and at what level. With a passive radiator, also: its volume displacement against the driver's, the sign of the required added mass, the null frequency, and whether the radiator or the cone reaches its limit first.
- **Where the sound leaves the box.** Which panel each driver and each outlet is on, and what that implies for a speaker used where the brief says it will be used. A radiating panel facing a nearby wall has a measured penalty, not a neutral one; see `framework/references/passive-radiators.md` section 4 and `framework/references/baffle-and-driver-placement.md` section 4.
- **The load the filter works into.** `compensation_audit` makes "the Zobel is right" checkable instead of assumed: residual variation under about 1 ohm or 10 percent across at least an octave either side of the crossover, and the **equalised effective resistance** the filter actually sees, which is the figure a component formula should have used rather than the nominal rating. `compensation_declared: false` means the section has no compensation at all, which at second order and above is a finding in itself. A residual that stays large after compensation is usually a semi-inductive driver a single R-C cannot track, not a wrong value.
- **Level and heat.** Each section's presence in its band; pad depth against the sensitivity mismatch it corrects; `insertion_loss.resistive_loss_db` for what the real parts cost as distinct from the attenuation the topology asked for; resistor dissipation with the drive condition named, against the rating for the mounting the board actually has rather than a rating that assumes a heatsink; a pad above about 10 dB is a component-selection finding. A crossover that would need cooling hardware, or resistors in the tens, is a finding about the driver complement and the system level, and it is reported that section rather than as a thermal detail.
- **Load.** Input impedance minimum and its frequency; phase angle near the minimum; behaviour in bi-amp modes when the board has separate inputs.
- **Robustness.** Tolerance envelopes of the crossover, alignment percentiles, depth perturbations. A design that only works at nominal values is fragile. Check the perturbation against the quantity it perturbs before quoting it: a depth shift larger than a driver's own acoustic depth is a probe of how steeply the model degrades, not a mounting scenario, and it cannot discriminate between candidates because it ruins all of them.
- **Cabinet.** First internal mode relative to the woofer's passband and crossover, cone deviation and outlet leakage, how much the lining helps, and for a wave-based `cavity_modes` run whether the finest mesh is inside `validity.mesh_rule`. For panels, quote the **band** `plate_structure` reports between pinned and clamped edges, never one frequency: the edge condition alone moves the fundamental by a factor of 1.8 to 2.2, a real glued joint is neither, and the BBC's own cabinet measured below even the pinned bound. Judge it against the audibility criterion, which is 13 dB of required margin at 150 Hz and 30 dB at 1 kHz - so a mode moved up in frequency by a thicker wall lands in the least forgiving part of the curve. The criterion carries `+/- 1 dB` of scatter and **no theoretical backing whatsoever**; say so with the figure, and never convert panel motion into an absolute SPL, which is not computable from the panels alone.
- **Whether the design can be documented.** The final package needs dimensioned interior geometry, a per-section acoustic phase relationship at a shared observation point, and a parameter file whose banks are individual parts; `speaker-documentation` says why. Where one of those is missing or ambiguous, report it as a finding with the concrete improvement - not as a new threshold, and not as a reason to reject a candidate. Check relative phase on a shared observation and delay reference, with the section levels in the overlap band beside it, and phrase anything that will reach the builder in terms a builder can act on.
- **What the network keeps.** `efficiency.loss_db` with `octave_bands[]` for where the loss sits, read together with the pad depths and the compensation audit, because the same figure comes from two unlike causes: attenuation a sensitivity mismatch forces, which is a driver-complement finding, and current a compensation branch diverts around a driver, which is a topology finding. There is no share that passes and none that fails; what the evaluation owes is the number, the cause and what changing it would cost. Where the ablation pass was run, say which elements the network is leaning on and which it can absorb, with `input_z_min_ohm` beside the delta, and treat a small delta as a question for the designer rather than an instruction to delete a part.
- **Construction and cost.** Part count from `parts.total` rather than from the schematic, with where the bodies went; the split between driver cost and crossover cost; whether the parts chosen are generally available, and whether a premium part was preferred over a cheaper one without a modelled difference to show for it - that is a finding, not a style note, and the answer is a rerun with the cheaper part's own figures. Stock availability, price against the budget, size against the limits, anything that cannot be built as drawn. Where the candidate claims that something fits inside the cabinet, or that something could not be fitted, check the derivation: the object's three ranges, the occupancy rows it would overlap, and the alternatives tried. A fit claim made on one coordinate, or on a driver treated as a solid block, is not yet a finding.
- **Model risk.** Which figures rest on datasheet priors (horn phase, coverage, impedance models) and which on the lumped models; where a measurement would change the answer most. Read each document's `validity` block and say where a figure was quoted outside the band its own model holds in - above the first internal standing wave for a lumped box, below the gate of an imported measurement, behind a cabinet whose rear radiation is a declared prior. No source gives a simulated-versus-measured error bar for any tool, so the validity band is the only bound available and quoting a number from outside it is the failure mode to catch.
- **Heat, where the level makes it matter.** A design whose corner frequencies or pad depth move audibly between 20 C and 100 C is a finding, not a detail: read `crossover_network`'s `coil_temperature_cases` together with `thermal_power`'s compression, because the network gives a hot driver more volts while the driver turns fewer of them into sound. Both are scenarios over priors no datasheet publishes; say so with the figure. A cored inductor's saturation current is **not published and has not been measured** - the supplier says so itself - so read `inductor_ratings.frequency_rule` instead: below about 1.2 kHz a ferromagnetic core is the vendor's own recommendation, above it a cored coil is a choice the candidate had to argue for, and every model here treats every coil as linear at every level. `thermal_power`'s priors come from the accelerated power-test protocol; nothing in the framework is calibrated against AES2-2012's two-hour endurance test, so no figure from it is a rated power in that sense.

## Compare

- Candidates against each other: a table of the objective figures and the descriptive statistics; state which differences are real (larger than the tolerance envelopes) and which are noise.
- Candidate against the reference: `response_metrics` comparison with and without level alignment, octave-band differences, sensitivity, extension, impedance, size, mass estimate, and the reference's character (directivity, alignment type). Say where the candidate is better, where worse, and what is unknown about the reference.
- Refinement lineage: for a candidate with a `parent`, state whether the requested change produced the expected effect, quoting before and after.

## Decide and request

Rank the candidates and say why in two or three sentences each. Then write refinement requests that a designer can act on without asking: the part or value or dimension to change, the figure it should move, and the expected magnitude. Requests caused by a component (shelved woofer, hot pad, coverage mismatch) ask for a different part from the inventory or for new inventory research, not for more crossover values. Requests caused by geometry (comb in the overlap band, floor dip) ask for a position or depth change.

State whether another iteration is worth spending: if no request can plausibly change the ranking or close a missed objective, recommend stopping and name the candidate for the final package with its open findings.

## Do not

- Do not soften a finding because the budget is nearly spent, and do not invent a threshold that is not in `project.json`; write the descriptive value and your judgement instead.
- Do not present a simulated figure as measured, and do not claim the model proves distortion, compression, pair matching or freedom from rattles.
- Do not modify candidate files or parameter files.

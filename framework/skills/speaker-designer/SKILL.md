# Speaker designer

You design one candidate at a time inside `framework/projects/<slug>/candidates/<cNN>/`. The simulation apps in `framework/simulations/` give you numbers; you make the engineering choices and write them down. Read `project.json` (objectives, limits, listening geometry), `reference/reference.json`, `inventory/screen.md`, and, for a refinement, the parent candidate and the evaluation that requested the change.

Every app: `.speaker-venv/bin/python framework/simulations/<app>/run.py --input candidates/cNN/sim/<name>.json --out candidates/cNN/sim --name <name>`. One command per run. Read each app's `README.md` before first use.

Before the first candidate, scan `framework/learnings/INDEX.md` for the validation and design phases and open the matching files (see `speaker-learnings`). They describe how earlier projects built wrong sources, misread levels or set the system level from the wrong section. Decide what applies here and log it; they are advice, not steps to follow verbatim.

`framework/references/` holds the engineering itself, and each one covers a decision you are about to make:

Learnings say what happened to earlier projects; references say what is true of loudspeakers and where it is written down. Neither carries a threshold for this brief.

## Order of work

1. **Intent.** Write `candidate.json` from `framework/templates/candidate.json` with the idea in one sentence: which parts, which alignment, what this candidate tries that others do not.

2. **Bass alignment (`lf_box`).** Work from `framework/references/enclosure-alignments.md`, and from `framework/references/driver-selection.md` for what the parameter set says the driver can do before any box. Start from the woofer's Thiele/Small data.

   **Read three blocks before you describe a box.** `results.efficiency` gives the reference efficiency the driver's own parameters fix and, in `ceiling`, the lossless limit this volume and extension cannot be taken above, with the margin below it; real systems land 2 to 3 dB under that ceiling, so a sensitivity target above it is a finding about the driver complement before it is a design problem. `results.displacement_limit` names which excursion limit binds - put the IEC 62458 figures in `driver.ts.displacement_limits` where the parts record has them, because inductance often binds well below a geometric Xmax - and `results.displacement_limited_output` turns that into the SPL the bass section supports, with the 12 dB span Small's own `kP` range covers. And ask `alignment` what box the driver wanted: it solves Small's lossy quartic at the `QL` you choose and reports the **required `QT` beside the driver's actual `Qts`**, so the gap between them is visible rather than discovered later as a shelved response.

   **Record which definition every `Xmax` came from.** `driver.ts.xmax_standard` is an enumeration - `geometric`, `aes2_1984`, `iec_62458`, `aes2_2012` or `not_stated` - because four definitions are in circulation and only the last two measure the same quantity. Free text is an input error, a blank becomes `not_stated`, and a figure without its definition cannot be compared with another driver's. Where the parts record has the IEC 62458 limits or an AES2-2012 measured figure, put them in `displacement_limits`; the app reports which binds and flags a set that mixes parameter-based with performance-based figures.

   **A loading other than sealed or vented is a decision to argue for, and the app makes the price visible.** Work from `framework/references/alternative-loadings.md` first. `driver.isobaric` transforms the driver record into its coupled-pair equivalent before any box maths: half the box at the same alignment, and `results.isobaric.exchange` states what it costs - **3 dB less efficiency, twice the power handling, two drivers, and the same maximum output as one**, so it buys volume with money and amplifier power and with nothing else. A `bandpass` enclosure is described as chambers rather than as an alignment, because **no bandpass alignment table was obtained for any family**; read `results.bandpass.exchange` for the bandwidth given up to get the midband gain, and `results.bandpass.port_resonances` for each port's first pipe resonance, above which the acoustic low-pass stops working and **an electrical low-pass is in practice mandatory**. An `aperiodic` vent needs at least two `rav_sweep_pa_s_per_m3` values and is labelled exploratory, because `RAV` has no published relation to any material; never report one row of that sweep as a designed alignment, and never quote a rolloff slope from it.

   Then explore volume and tuning with several runs; read `statistics.cutoffs`, `impedance_min_ohm`, `max_excursion_peak_mm` and `max_port_air_speed_peak_ms` at the stress voltage of the project, and `linear_scaling_to_limits`. Design guidance, not rules: a vented alignment with Qts around .3 to .4 and tuning near the target f6 sits shelved a few dB below the midband, which the reference comparison will show; the port air speed ceiling depends on duct diameter and is not a single number, because compression sets in at a Reynolds number near 50,000 rather than at a fixed velocity - that is about `0.75 / D` m/s peak with `D` in metres, so roughly 15 m/s in a 50 mm duct, 7.5 m/s in a 100 mm one and 5 m/s in a 150 mm one, and the old flat "15 to 20 m/s" habit is far too permissive for any large duct (see `framework/references/enclosure-alignments.md` section 4). `results.vent_physics` now computes all of that for the duct in hand: the Reynolds number at the reported peak velocity, which of the two thresholds binds for this diameter, Small's minimum vent area, and the `>= 0.2 Dmin` edge radius. Give `enclosure.port.flare_radius_mm`, `throat_diameter_mm` and `length_mm` for a flared duct and read the tuning it actually produces - a flared port of the same throat area needs more length than a straight one to reach the same tuning, and without those keys its tuning is an unflagged estimate. Excursion at the stress drive stays inside the **binding** displacement limit with margin because the model is linear. Consider a passive radiator when the reference uses one or when the duct would be impractically long; then work from `framework/references/passive-radiators.md`, because a radiator is sized by volume displacement before it is chosen by diameter, and read the sign of `results.radiator.required_added_mass_g` before you read the curve - a negative value means the alignment cannot be built with that radiator in that volume, and the response is computed anyway. Run `lf_uncertainty` when the alignment sits near an edge you care about.

3. **Cabinet.** Work from `framework/references/cabinet-geometry.md`. Convert the net volume into outside dimensions inside the size limits: `framework/tools/cabinet_drawing.py` gives the chamber net volume after braces, port tubes, lining and driver displacement. Iterate until net volume matches the alignment. Place braces so that no side bay is longer than about 300 to 400 mm, keep the port inner ends away from the woofer and the walls, and keep the upper drivers in a separate chamber when they are cones or domes. Keep an interior occupancy list in `notes.md`, one row per object with its width, depth and height ranges and where each dimension came from: nothing in the tools checks whether two objects share space, so both "it fits" and "it does not fit" are derivations from that list rather than impressions from one elevation. Keep that list dimensioned rather than descriptive - position and thickness of every partition, brace and lining region, from the same numbers the volume accounting used - because `speaker-documentation` draws the interior from it, and a simplification made for the acoustic model is recorded separately from the physical geometry.

4. **Sections (`source_response`).** One file per section on the same grid (10 to 40000 Hz, 800 points): the woofer from the `lf_box` cone pressure with the datasheet shape above the blend band; horn and tweeter from digitized datasheet curves or bandwidth priors with their sensitivity; the impedance model from the datasheet (resonant prior for compression drivers and domes, ribbon prior for ribbons, box impedance for the woofer). Record the acoustic depth of each section from drawings: horn throat depth plus driver, roughly 15 to 30 mm for a dome or ribbon, about the cone depth for a woofer.

5. **Placement, before the baffle run.** Work from `framework/references/baffle-and-driver-placement.md` and `framework/references/system-architecture.md` and write the placement table into `notes.md`: for each driver its frame and cutout diameter, its centre height, its distance to the two nearest baffle edges, its acoustic depth, its centre-to-centre spacing `d` to each neighbour and the frequency `c / (2d)` above which that pair can null, and the woofer's distance to the floor against the half-panel-dimension rule. Put a passive radiator on a panel chosen for a reason from that reference's section 4, and name the reason. Do this before choosing crossover frequencies, not after: a crossover picked first simply reappears as a vertical window figure later.

6. **Baffle (`baffle_diffraction`).** Front panel dimensions and each source position; apply the factor to the woofer, to any rear outlet, and to any section whose datasheet curve was not taken on a comparable baffle. Read `relative_to_2pi_db` as a curve, not as a corner frequency. Set `observer_distance_m` to the distance the correction will be used at and read `sources[].step`: the step is shallower close to the baffle than in the far field, so a correction derived at one distance under- or over-compensates at another by exactly that difference. Give the panel's `edge_radius_mm` when there is a roundover, and read `edge_radius_effective_above_hz` - below that frequency the edge is acoustically square whatever it looks like. How much of the step to compensate is a voicing decision to state: the full 6 dB belongs to free space and a room restores enough that about 3 dB is the practical figure.

7. **Directivity check (`directivity`).** Woofer beamwidth against horn or dome coverage at the planned lower crossover; move the crossover or change the part when they differ by more than you accept. Give a waveguide section an `os_waveguide` block and the app derives its contour and its cutoff `fc = 0.2 c sin(theta0) / (pi rt)` from the throat radius and half angle, and says whether the contour's included angle matches the coverage the section declares.

7a. **Horn sections (`horn_design`), before committing to one.** Work from `framework/references/horn-and-waveguide-design.md`. The whole design set is closed form over parameters the parts record already holds, and the first line settles the question: **`VB` is finite and positive only when `fs Qts < fc`**, so a driver that fails the suitability test is not suited to a horn at that cutoff and no cavity volume will annul the reactance. Then read the throat area (the efficiency-optimal one, which Keele himself says is not automatically the right one), the **rear cavity volume - which is what sets a bass horn's size and is usually the reason the design is abandoned**, the usable band `fHS/fLS = 1/Qts^2`, the three HF corners, and `PAR` for the output the displacement allows down to `1.26 fc`. The mouth criterion is reported against **both** published optima, 0.93 and 1.4, because the sources disagree by a factor of 2.3 in area and the app does not choose. Run `horn_loading` afterwards if you want the impedance of the contour you chose, and read its curves knowing that shape moves impedance by about a dB in the band where the equation is valid at all.

8. **Crossover.** Work from `framework/references/crossover-design.md` for the network and `framework/references/target-response.md` for what you are fitting toward, and settle the level budget before any value: each section's pad depth is its in-band sensitivity minus the system level the bass section supports, and a large one is a finding about the driver complement, not a resistor to size. Choose the topology per section (second to fourth order electrical ladders, pad for the sensitive section, Zobel for the woofer, notch where a datasheet peak sits). Give every cored coil a `core` and read `inductor_ratings`: the saturation current of a cored coil is **not published and has not been measured** - the supplier says so itself - so the field carries that string rather than a blank, the row names the core's failure mode, and `frequency_rule` checks the section's passband against the vendor's own 1.2 kHz threshold. A cored coil above it is a choice to argue for in the notes; below it, it is their own recommendation. Mark every Zobel and notch with `"role": "compensation"` and give the section a `compensation_audit: {crossover_hz, octaves}`, so the audit can report the residual impedance variation against the roughly 1 ohm / 10 percent target and the equalised effective resistance the filter actually works into - which is the number a component formula should use, never the nominal rating on the label. Give every element an `id` and bounds, and run `crossover_fit` with the coherent-sum objective on axis and a slightly off-axis vertical angle, a target level, and the impedance minimum you want to protect. Read `fitted_values` and `values_at_bound`: a value on a bound means the topology or bound is wrong, not that the optimizer is done. Then quantize to stock values from `inventory/stock/`, put the real DCR and ESR in, add `part` (manufacturer and series) and `rating` (capacitor voltage, resistor power, coil current) to every leaf so the schematic and parts list can name them. Where a position is a bank, write it as the individual parts it is built from rather than as one equivalent value: that parameter file is what the construction schematic and the wiring table are drawn from, and stress is reported per physical part, so a bank written as one value hides both. Then run `crossover_network` with tolerance draws at the stress voltage. Read component stress (`components[].max_dissipation_w`) against the stock ratings for the mounting this board actually has - a rating that assumes a heatsink does not apply to a board in a sealed box - and state the drive condition next to the figure. Read the input impedance minimum with its frequency and phase, and each section's transfer. Read `insertion_loss` for what the real parts cost in level as distinct from the attenuation the topology asked for, `inductor_ratings` for any coil whose saturation current nobody published (a cored coil in a woofer branch with no such rating is a risk to state, since the model treats every coil as linear at every level), and - where the project's level makes heat matter - give `sections[].re_ohm` and a `thermal.coil_temperatures_c` pair so the design can be read cold and hot.

9. **Whole speaker (`whole_speaker`).** Read `results.pair_geometry` first: it is arithmetic over positions you already chose, so the `c / (2d)` null frequency, the `c / d` spacing bound and the lobe tilt the depth offsets imply were all available at step 5. If a crossing sits above a pair's `spacing_bound_hz`, that is the placement, not the filter. All sections with their transfers, baffle factors and positions; rear outlets for the port or radiator; horizontal and vertical angles; floor cases from the project's listening geometry; `polarity_study` on. Set `depth_perturbation_mm` from the acoustic depths in play rather than from the default: the application applies one figure to every section, and a shift larger than a driver's own acoustic depth is a probe of how steeply the model degrades, not a mounting scenario. Run two values and say which is which. Declare an `arrays` block when two or more sources reproduce the **same** band - the woofers of an MTM, a stacked pair, a column - because this model sums sections and a driver pair inside one section is a single point source to it. `results.arrays.groups[]` gives the `lambda/2` criterion as a pass or fail with `c / (2 d)`, **the number that should have set that group's crossover before any fitting**, and the Active Radiating Factor against the 80 percent criterion that touching circular sources cannot meet. Give `arrays.coherent_radiating_height_mm` and `results.arrays.near_field` reports the near/far-field border against the distance this result was computed at: inside it level falls 3 dB per doubling instead of 6 and **a polar is not a far-field property at all**, which for a 1 m coherent section means about 1.4 m at 1 kHz and about 15 m at 10 kHz. Read `statistics` (ripple, sensitivity mean, cutoffs, acoustic crossings, section presence), the vertical window deviation, and the polarity study. If a polarity flip gives a smoother sum, change the polarity in the crossover and rerun `crossover_network` so the two files agree. Keep the run that produced the final sum: its per-section complex pressure at one observation point, with the crossover, polarity, baffle and propagation terms in it, is what the documentation's phase plots are made from, and electrical filter phase is not a substitute for it.

10. **Metrics (`response_metrics`).** The candidate's on-axis curve against the reference's digitized curve with and without level alignment, plus the statistics the evaluator needs, using the objective bands from `project.json`. Every curve also gets NBD, SM and AAD, which separate local deviation from broad tilt the way the preference research does. **Name each curve's kind with `curves[].curve`** so the metrics come back as `NBD_ON` and `SM_PIR` rather than after the curve's own label: the definitions are each defined over any of the seven CTA curves, so a bare NBD names nothing. The definitions themselves are confirmed at first hand in Harman's patent; what is approximate is the curve set, which is why they are labelled `approximation` and must never be reported as CTA-2034-A figures.

10a. **Directivity and preference (`spinorama`), where the design is judged on its pattern.** Take the `whole_speaker` parameter document, add a `rear_radiation` prior to every section and to any rear outlet, and run it. It computes the listening window, the five early-reflection groups, sound power, the predicted in-room curve, both directivity indices and the Olive preference score. Without a prior on every section the orbit is incomplete and the score is withheld deliberately, because two of the four preference terms are directivity measurements. The prior is a declared assumption with no measurement behind it and the sound power carries about a third of its weight in the 90-degree ring alone, so say what you assumed with any figure you quote from it. Keep `options` at the defaults, which are the standard's literal reading, unless you are deliberately comparing against a tool that uses the other convention.

11. **Detailed analysis, finalists only.** `thermal_power` where the brief's level makes coil heating matter, feeding its steady-state coil temperature back into `lf_box` (`thermal.coil_temperature_c`) and `crossover_network` (`thermal.coil_temperatures_c`); its priors come from the accelerated power-test protocol, so nothing it reports is a rated power in the AES2-2012 endurance sense and the two protocols are printed side by side for that reason. `cavity_modes` with the FVM basis and two meshes - read `validity.mesh_rule` for the maximum cell size `h = c P / (10 f_max)` and whether the finest mesh is inside it. `plate_structure` fed from its pressure envelope scaled to the stress voltage, with a `material.name` from its table and the `treatments` you intend: it reports each panel's fundamental as a **band between pinned and clamped edges** rather than one frequency, because the edge condition alone moves it by a factor of 1.8 to 2.2, and it overlays the BBC's measured audibility criterion - 13 dB of margin needed at 150 Hz, 30 dB at 1 kHz - on a relative panel output. Give `audibility.cone` an excursion curve and `sd_cm2` to get that comparison at all. Then `framework/tools/schematic.py` to derive a board netlist, then `board_nodal` with the wiring resistances you expect and all connection modes; feed the linked-mode load transfers back into `whole_speaker` to see the board rather than the ideal ladders.

12. **Record.** Fill `candidate.json` completely: parts, enclosure, cabinet, placement, crossover file references, simulation file list, designer notes (what you tried, what you would change next, which numbers rest on priors), estimated cost from inventory prices.

## How to think

- The speaker is judged as a whole. A sensitivity reached by padding the horn 12 dB is heat, not efficiency; a woofer that is shelved 4 dB below the horn cannot be fixed with coils. Change the part.
- Every result document carries a `validity` block saying the band in which its model holds, and `constants` and `version` saying what produced it. Quote a figure from outside its own validity band and the evaluator will say so; give `enclosure.internal_dimensions_mm` and `acoustic.gate_ms` where you have them, because those are what let the applications state the bound at all.
- Three applications answer a loading question before it is committed to, and nothing downstream consumes them: `horn_loading` for whether a horn loads its driver and whether its mouth delivers the pattern on the label, `open_baffle` for what a dipole would cost in displacement, and `transmission_line` for a line's length and whether it is worth building at all - its own source concludes that at equal volume and tuning it gives no deeper bass than a reflex box.
- Crossover points belong inside the published ranges of both neighbouring drivers with margin, and where the woofer's beamwidth is close to the horn's coverage.
- Path differences between acoustic centres show up as the comb in the overlap band; move the driver or change the depth before adding parts.
- A curve is judged by the kind of its deviations, not by their peak-to-peak spread. A gentle broad tilt is cheap; a step at a crossover, a hump-and-dip pair across an octave, or a local feature at the baffle step or the box tuning is expensive, and a ripple figure over a decade cannot tell them apart. Read the slope and the deviation about it, per band, and see `framework/references/target-response.md`.
- Fewer parts at real tolerances usually beat more parts. Compare tolerance envelopes, not only nominal curves.
- The interior is three dimensional and its occupants are shaped. Two objects clash only when their width, depth and height ranges all overlap; a cone driver is a flange, a frustum and a magnet, not a block the size of its frame; a horn sits at the front of its chamber and a rear duct at the back. A clash proved for one object proves nothing about its neighbour, so derive each one.
- Heat has nowhere to go inside a passive box. A network that would need a heatsink, a fan or resistors by the dozen is reporting that the level budget is wrong; the fix is a different driver complement or a different system level, not a cooling arrangement.
- Report what the model cannot tell: distortion, compression, port noise, breakup beyond the datasheet, cabinet joint behaviour.

## Refinement requests

When the evaluation asks for a change, make the change in a new candidate with `parent` set, keep everything else identical unless the request says otherwise, rerun the affected apps and every downstream app, and state in `notes.md` what changed in which figure and whether the request's expectation held.

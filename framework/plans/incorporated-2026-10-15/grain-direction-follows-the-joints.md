## What went wrong
A solid-birch sleeve had wrap-around grain, chosen so that the opening stayed stable and only the depth moved with the seasons; its end-grain mitres therefore needed splines. When the splines were removed at the user's request, the mitres became end-grain glue joints. Changing the grain to front-to-back made them long grain, but that also turned the narrow lips in front of and behind the panel grooves into short grain. It moved the captured panels' float gap from the sides to the top and bottom. None of this was in the design analysis; it surfaced while the drawings were reworked.

## What could have helped
Treat grain direction, joint type and the captured panels' gaps as one decision, and recheck all three whenever one changes. For each candidate grain direction, list:
- whether each joint is long or end grain;
- which dimension of the opening moves, and how much over the expected moisture range;
- which gaps the captured panels need, in which direction;
- which narrow edges become short grain.
Then choose. State the resulting machining and handling cautions in the cut list. The movement coefficients and gap sizes depend on the species and climate, so take them from wood data, not from another project.
